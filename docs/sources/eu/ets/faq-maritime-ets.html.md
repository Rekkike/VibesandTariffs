## Operative content

**Timing and scope.** The EU ETS has been extended to maritime transport
emissions since 1 January 2024; the new rules entered into force on 5 June
2023. A phased approach for surrendering obligations: "starting at 40% in
2024, 70% in 2025 and reaching 100% by 2026 for the ship types under
scope" (the reporting-year formulation on the timeline; the surrender-year
formulation below).

**Ships in scope.** Since 2024: cargo and passenger ships of or above 5 000
gross tonnage (GT). From 2027: offshore ships of or above 5 000 GT. (MRV
scope from 2025 additionally covers offshore and general cargo ships below
5 000 GT but not below 400 GT; inclusion of such ships in ETS is under
review, with a Commission report due by 31 December 2026.)

**Gases.** MRV covers CO2, CH4 (since 2024), N2O (since 2024). The ETS
covers CO2 in the first two years (2024 and 2025 reported emissions), with
CH4 and N2O falling under ETS scope starting 2026 (reported emissions).

**Emissions in scope (route-based, flag-neutral).**

- 100% of emissions from ships performing voyages departing from a port
  under the jurisdiction of an EU Member State and arriving at a port under
  the jurisdiction of an EU Member State (e.g. Hamburg to Marseille and
  Marseille to Hamburg);
- 100% of emissions from ships within a port under the jurisdiction of an
  EU Member State (e.g. in the port of Antwerp), i.e. emissions released
  at berth and during movements within such a port;
- 50% of emissions from voyages departing from a port under the
  jurisdiction of an EU Member State and arriving at a port outside its
  jurisdiction (e.g. Rotterdam to Shanghai);
- 50% of the emissions from voyages departing from a port outside the
  jurisdiction of an EU Member State and arriving at a port under its
  jurisdiction (e.g. Shanghai to Rotterdam).

Some derogations apply (outermost regions, small islands, renewable
fuels).

**Port of call.** The port where a ship stops to load or unload cargo, to
embark or disembark passengers, or where an offshore ship stops to relieve
the crew. Excluded stops: refuelling; obtaining supplies; crew relief
(other than offshore ships); dry-dock or repairs; assistance or distress;
ship-to-ship transfers outside ports; shelter or search-and-rescue;
containership stops at listed neighbouring container transhipment ports.

**Emissions within ports.** Emissions at berth plus emissions from
movements within the port of call; emissions related to entry into or exit
from the port before or after the port of call are part of the voyage, not
"emissions within ports".

**When allowances are surrendered.** First ETS allowances by 30 September
2025 for emissions reported in 2024. The share gradually increases:

- 2025: 40% of emissions reported for 2024 must be covered by emission
  allowances;
- 2026: 70% of emissions reported for 2025;
- 2027 and beyond: 100% of reported emissions.

**How the ETS works.** Cap-and-trade; one allowance gives the right to
emit one tonne of CO2eq; a company emitting 10,000 tonnes of in-scope CO2
during a reporting period needs to buy and surrender 10,000 allowances by
30 September of the following year. Allowances are auctioned (EEX) and
traded; there is no free allocation to maritime.

**MRV basis.** Monitoring per approved monitoring plans; annual emissions
reports per ship and aggregated at company level; verified by accredited
verifiers (by 31 March of the following year, or 28 February if requested
by the administering authority); surrender by 30 September. Data reported
through THETIS-MRV (EMSA-operated).

**Union Registry.** Shipping companies open Maritime Operator Holding
Accounts (MOHA); general EU allowances (EUA) — the same instruments as
other ETS sectors; no maritime-specific allowance type.

**Company responsibility.** The shipping company (the ISM company, or the
registered owner by default) is responsible for surrendering; the same
entity handles MRV; administering authorities per Article 3gf (registered
country for EU companies; greatest port-call count over the last four
monitoring years for non-EU companies). Companies are entitled to
reimbursement from the entity responsible for the operation of the ship
(determining cargo, route, and speed) — Member States enforce that
entitlement.
