## Operative content

**ETS maritime inclusion.** Since January 2024, the EU ETS covers CO2
emissions from all large ships (of 5 000 gross tonnage and above) entering
EU ports, regardless of flag. The system covers:

- 50% of emissions from voyages starting or ending outside of the EU;
- 100% of emissions that occur between two EU ports and when ships are
  within EU ports.

The EU ETS covers CO2, CH4 and N2O emissions, the latter two only as from
2026. Emissions from maritime transport are included in the overall ETS
cap. Shipping companies have to purchase and surrender emission allowances
for each tonne of reported CO2 (or CO2 equivalent) in scope.

**Phase-in.** Shipping companies only have to surrender allowances for a
portion of their emissions during the initial phase-in period:

- 2025: for 40% of their emissions reported in 2024;
- 2026: for 70% of their emissions reported in 2025;
- 2027 onwards: for 100% of their reported emissions.

The first surrendering deadline falls due in September 2025 in all Member
States, in respect of emissions reported as taking place from 1 January
2024 to 31 December 2024. These rules were adopted on 16 May 2023 and
entered into force on 5 June 2023.

The legal texts below the page: the amendments to the MRV Maritime
Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2015/757) — consolidated version — and the
amendments to the ETS Directive (Directive 2003/87/EC): **Directive (EU)
2023/959 of 10 May 2023**. Implementing and delegated acts include
Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/2297 (neighbouring container transhipment
ports), Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/2449 (templates), Implementing
Regulation (EU) 2023/2599 (administration of shipping companies), Delegated
Regulation (EU) 2023/2849 (company-level aggregated emissions data
reporting), Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/2776 (monitoring rules),
Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/2917 (verification/accreditation), and
Implementing Decision (EU) 2023/2895 (islands and transnational public
service contracts).

**MRV.** Since 1 January 2018, large ships over 5 000 gross tonnage loading
or unloading cargo or passengers at EEA ports must monitor and report GHG
emissions (CO2; CH4 and N2O as of 1 January 2024) through THETIS-MRV;
reports verified by accredited verifiers; documents of compliance carried
on board. The Commission publishes an annual MRV report on the monitored
fleet.

**FuelEU companion.** The page also records the FuelEU Maritime Regulation
(Regulation (EU) 2023/1805) as part of the Fit-for-55 maritime basket: a
GHG-intensity limit on energy used by ships calling at European ports and
the OPS-at-berth obligation for container and passenger ships from 2030.
