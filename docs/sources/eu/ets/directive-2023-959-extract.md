## What the Directive does

It inserts Articles 3ga–3gg into Directive 2003/87/EC (the ETS Directive),
extending the EU ETS to maritime transport from 1 January 2024 (per the
Commission ETS FAQ E2 and the maritime page E1). The article structure, as
published in the Commission's own documentation and the EUR-Lex TOC:

- **Article 3ga** — Scope of application to maritime transport activities
- **Article 3gb** — Phase-in of requirements for maritime transport
- **Article 3gc** — Provisions for transfer of the costs of the EU ETS from
  the shipping company to another entity
- **Article 3gd** — Monitoring and reporting of emissions from maritime
  transport
- **Article 3ge** — Verification and accreditation rules for emissions from
  maritime transport
- **Article 3gf** — Administering authority in respect of a shipping company
- **Article 3gg** — Reporting and review

## The operative provisions (as quoted by the official Commission sources)

### Scope (Article 3ga)

From the Commission's maritime-ETS FAQ (E2), which restates the Directive's
scope:

- Applies since 2024 to cargo and passenger ships **of or above 5 000 gross
  tonnage (GT)**, regardless of flag. (From 2027: offshore ships ≥5,000 GT.)
- Covers 100% of emissions from ships performing voyages departing from a
  port under the jurisdiction of an EU Member State and arriving at a port
  under the jurisdiction of an EU Member State (both directions);
- 100% of emissions from ships within a port under the jurisdiction of an
  EU Member State (emissions at berth and during movements within such a
  port);
- 50% of emissions from voyages departing an EU Member State port and
  arriving at a port outside its jurisdiction, and 50% of voyages arriving
  from outside into an EU port.
- "Port of call" means the port where a ship stops to load or unload cargo,
  to embark or disembark passengers, or where an offshore ship stops to
  relieve the crew; excluded are stops solely for refuelling, obtaining
  supplies, relieving the crew (other than offshore ships), dry-docking or
  repairs, distress/assistance, ship-to-ship transfers outside ports,
  shelter, search-and-rescue-necessary stops, and stops of containerships
  at the neighbouring container transhipment ports listed in the
  implementing act (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/2297).
- GHG scope: CO2 for 2024- and 2025-reported emissions; CH4 and N2O added
  for 2026-reported emissions (MRV monitors all three from 2024).

### Phase-in of the surrender obligation (Article 3gb)

From the Commission FAQ (E2), restating Article 3gb(1):

- 2025: shipping companies surrender allowances for **40 % of verified
  emissions reported for 2024** that would be subject to surrender
  requirements in accordance with Article 12;
- 2026: **70 % of verified emissions reported for 2025**;
- 2027 and beyond: **100 %** of reported emissions.

First surrender deadline 30 September 2025 (for 2024 emissions); annual
thereafter. Where fewer allowances are surrendered than the verified
emissions in 2024/2025, the difference is cancelled from auctioning
(Articles 3gb and 12(3-e), per the Commission's 2024 carbon-market report).

### Surrender and penalties

One allowance = one tonne of CO2-equivalent; the shipping company
responsible for the ship under ISM (or the registered owner by default)
surrenders in the Union Registry (Maritime Operator Holding Account) to its
administering authority (Article 3gf); non-compliance carries the excess
emissions penalty (EUR 100/tCO2eq, inflation-linked, per the ETS Directive's
Article 16 as applied to maritime).

### Cost pass-through (Article 3gc)

Member States ensure shipping companies are entitled to reimbursement from
the entity responsible for the ship's operation (fuel purchase / route and
speed determination) for ETS costs — the directive's own cost-allocation
rule between company and commercial operator.
