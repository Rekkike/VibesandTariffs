## Operative content

Regulation (EU) 2023/1805 (FuelEU maritime) is fully applied from 1 January
2025 except for the monitoring-plan articles (8 and 9), applied from
August 2024.

**Key facts.** FuelEU maritime sets maximum limits for the **yearly average
greenhouse gas (GHG) intensity of the energy used by ships above 5,000
gross tonnage calling at European ports, regardless of their flag**.
Targets decrease over time: "Annual average carbon intensity reduction
compared to the average in 2020:

- 2025: -2%;
- 2030: -6%;
- 2035: -14.5%;
- 2040: -31%;
- 2045: -62%;
- 2050: -80%."

The targets cover CO2, methane and nitrous oxide over the full lifecycle of
the fuels used on board, on a well-to-wake (WtW) basis.

**Zero emissions at berth.** Passenger and container ships at berth or
moored at the quayside must use on-shore power supply (OPS) or alternative
zero-emission technologies from 1 January 2030 onwards in ports covered
under article 9 of the Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Regulation (AFIR),
and from 1 January 2035 in all EU ports that develop OPS capacity; Member
States may apply the obligation to ports not covered by AFIR article 9 from
1 January 2030.

**Fleet facts stated on the page.** Vessels >5000 gross tonnes = 55% of all
ships = 90% of CO2 emissions from the maritime sector.

**Reporting calendar.** The reporting period is the year monitored; first
reporting period 2025; verification period the year after; the first
FuelEU report submission to a verifier is due on 31 January 2026,
containing the data from 1 January 2025 to 31 December 2025. Monitoring
records (from 1 January 2025) include ports of departure and arrival with
dates/times and duration at berth, OPS connection or exceptions, fuel
consumption at berth and at sea, electricity received through OPS,
well-to-tank and tank-to-wake emission factors per fuel, substitute energy
consumed at berth and at sea, and ice-class information.

**Secondary legislation listed.** Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/394
(FuelEU database), Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1127 (neighbouring
container transhipment ports), Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2027
(verification activities), Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2031
(monitoring plan template), Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/192
(accreditation of verifiers).

**EEA note.** FuelEU delayed in Norway and Iceland until EEA incorporation
(announced 18.12.2025): Norwegian and Icelandic ports are regarded as
third-country ports in a FuelEU context from 1 January 2025 until
incorporation — a boundary case that does not affect the three in-scope
ports (GOT, HAM, HEL).

**Full regulation link**: Regulation (EU) 2023/1805 of 13 September 2023
on the use of renewable and low-carbon fuels in maritime transport, and
amending Directive 2009/16/EC (EUR-Lex).
