## What the Regulation does

It sets (1) a limit on the yearly average greenhouse gas intensity of the
energy used on board ships calling at EU/EEA ports (Article 4), (2) a
zero-emissions-at-berth requirement for container and passenger ships via
OPS or zero-emission technologies (Article 6, from 2030 at AFIR-covered
ports, 2035 at equipped ports), and (3) an RFNBO incentive/subtarget
(Article 5). It builds on the MRV Maritime Regulation (2015/757 as amended
by 2023/957) for monitoring and verification, administered through
THETIS-MRV and the FuelEU database.

## The operative provisions (as quoted by the official Commission sources)

### Scope (Article 2)

From the Commission FuelEU Q&A (E6): applies to all commercial ships
**above 5,000 gross tonnes** transporting passengers or cargo,
irrespective of flag, in respect of:

- the entirety of the energy used on voyages between ports under the
  jurisdiction of Member States (intra-EU voyages);
- one half of the energy used on voyages starting or ending in a port
  under the jurisdiction of a Member State and the other port under the
  jurisdiction of a third country (international voyages), and voyages to
  or from outermost regions;
- (energy at berth in EU ports is in scope under the GHG-intensity
  calculation).

A "port of call" (Article 3(10)) is a port where the ship stops to load or
unload cargo or embark/disembark passengers; refuelling, supplies,
crew relief (non-offshore), dry-dock/repairs, distress, shelter, and
ship-to-ship transfers outside ports are excluded, as are containership
stops at listed neighbouring container transhipment ports.

### The GHG-intensity limit (Article 4)

From the Commission FuelEU page (E5) and Q&A (E6):

- The limit is on the **yearly average GHG intensity of the energy used on
  board**, on a well-to-wake basis (CO2, CH4, N2O over the fuel lifecycle).
- The **reference value is 91.16 gCO2e/MJ** — the 2020 fleet average
  computed from the THETIS-MRV 2020 reporting (Article 4(2); the Q&A's
  Question 4.1 records the basis and that the value is not subject to
  revision).
- Target reductions against the reference value (Article 4(2)):
  **2025–2029: −2%** (limit 89.34 gCO2e/MJ); 2030–2034: −6%; 2035–2039:
  −14.5%; 2040–2044: −31%; 2045–2049: −62%; 2050 onwards: −80%.

### Compliance balance, flexibility, and penalty (Articles 20–23, Annex IV)

From the Commission FuelEU Q&A (E6):

- The ship's **compliance balance** (Annex IV Part A) measures over- or
  under-compliance against the yearly limit, in gCO2e terms over the
  ship's in-scope energy.
- **Banking and borrowing** (Article 20): surplus balances may be banked to
  the following reporting period; a limited deficit may be borrowed from
  the next period (not in two consecutive periods).
- **Pooling** (Article 21): compliance balances may be pooled across ships
  (one pool per balance type; the verifier records the pool composition
  and allocation by 30 April of the verification period).
- **Penalty** (Article 23 and Annex IV Part B): a compliance deficit is
  penalised at a fixed rate expressed as **2,400 EUR per tonne of VLSFO
  equivalent energy** — the deficit converted to non-compliant energy and
  priced at the VLSFO-equivalent rate (≈ 0.058 EUR per MJ of
  non-compliant energy, per the ABS FAQ restatement). Consecutive-period
  deficits attract an increased penalty (Article 23(2) multiplier). A
  FuelEU penalty is also imposed for each non-compliant port call under
  the Article 6 berth requirement.
- The penalty is a fixed remedial price, distinct from the ETS's
  market-priced allowances; the two instruments are complementary (the
  Q&A's Question 1.3: ETS prices emissions quantity; FuelEU prices fuel
  intensity).

### Reporting calendar (Article 15)

Monitoring from 1 January 2025; FuelEU report to the verifier by 31
January each year; the verifier records it in the FuelEU database by 31
March; document of compliance thereafter. First cycle: 2025 data reported
January 2026.
