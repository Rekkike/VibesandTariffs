## The article in force

**A directive-premise correction, reported: the maritime shore-side
electricity obligation is Article 9 of Regulation (EU) 2023/1804** —
"Targets for shore-side electricity supply in maritime ports". AFIR's
Article 8 is infrastructure for liquefied methane for road transport
vehicles. The directive said "Art. 8"; the act's own article table prevails.

## Article 9 — Targets for shore-side electricity supply in maritime ports

- Member States shall ensure that a **minimum shore-side electricity
  supply for seagoing container ships and seagoing passenger ships** is
  provided in **TEN-T maritime ports**.
- Capability deadline: Member States shall take the necessary measures to
  ensure the deployment **by 31 December 2029**.
- Minimum supply: from **1 January 2030**, the shore-side electricity
  supply must satisfy at least **90% of the port calls** by the ships in
  scope, moored at the quayside.
- Traffic thresholds attaching the obligation per port (determined on the
  average annual number of port calls over the **last three years**, ships
  **above 5,000 GT** moored at the quayside):
  - container ships: **more than 100 calls/year**;
  - ro-ro passenger ships: **more than 40 calls/year**;
  - cruise ships: **more than 25 calls/year**.
- Port calls of the ships exempt under Regulation (EU) 2023/1805
  Article 6(5)(a),(b),(c),(e),(g) are not counted toward the threshold.
- The obligation binds Member States (the port-side infrastructure
  mandate); **AFIR levies no vessel-side charge** — the ship-side
  obligation to connect is FuelEU Maritime's (Regulation (EU) 2023/1805
  Article 6, from 1 January 2030 for container and passenger ships of
  5,000 GT and above at TEN-T quaysides, with the AFIR-compatibility and
  grid-stability exemptions the FuelEU text provides).
- Member States must also ensure sufficient grid infrastructure, power
  reserve, and frequency conversion is made available to meet the
  requirements (the grid-capacity sentence).
- Island/outermost-region carve-outs apply (ports not connected to the
  mainland grid, etc.) — none of the three modeled ports qualifies.

## Per-port facts (the three modeled ports)

- **Gothenburg (GOT)** — TEN-T core port. The OPS pioneer: the first Swedish
  high-voltage OPS installation (2000, with Stora Enso), Stena Line's RoRo
  OPS backbone since the 2000s, and the tanker-jet OPS concept at the Energy
  Terminal (first tanker connected 2024). OPS is operational at the RoRo,
  RoPax, and Energy terminals today; a new 19 MVA transformer station
  (SEK 129m contract signed 2025) enables OPS at **five container berths
  and two car/RoRo berths by 2030** (total container/car-terminal OPS
  investment ~SEK 600m, EU CEF co-financed ~SEK 90m). The port's own
  published aim is shore power for container and car/RoRo vessels
  "before 2030". Container traffic (~700k TEU/year of regular line
  services) far exceeds the 100-call threshold. Status: on track ahead of
  the deadline.
- **Hamburg (HAM)** — TEN-T core port. OPS operational since May 2024:
  the Eurogate CTH plant (three mega-ship berths, 7.5 MVA each, renewable
  supply) live, HHLA CTT following, with CTB and CTA in build — the port's
  stated completion is **all large container terminals equipped by the end
  of 2025** (ten shore-power connections for container ships plus four for
  cruise ships across CTH, CTB, CTT, CTA, Steinwerder, and HafenCity).
  Container traffic far exceeds the threshold. Status: capability in place
  ahead of the 2029 deadline.
- **Helsingborg (HEL)** — TEN-T comprehensive network port. OPS already
  offered for ferries at the City Port; Scandinavia's first
  container-vessel OPS facility announced 2025 (developed with Actemium,
  up to 3.5 MW — a feeder container vessel's needs), **commissioning
  planned autumn 2026**, driven by a 2019 Environmental Permit Authority
  ruling (shore power available for container vessels within seven years).
  Container calls (feeder line services) are the relevant threshold basis;
  the port's own announcements treat the 2030 mandate as applying. Status:
  in build, ahead of the deadline.

## Relevance to the model

AFIR is regulatory **context** for why OPS exists and is being tariffed at
these ports — the same treatment the ETS directive received (the v0.2.69
regulatory block). AFIR levies no vessel-side charge; the model's OPS
surfaces are the existing OPS speculation inputs (no published
container-terminal OPS rate exists at any of the three modeled ports —
verified at the v0.2.57/v0.2.64 passes; unchanged this pass). The AFIR
notes rendered at the OPS surfaces are zero-amount, never additive, never
a fee rule.
